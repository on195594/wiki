## Source thesis

The article's central claim is that AI-assisted mathematical experimentation is becoming much cheaper because candidate approaches can be generated in parallel and rejected with exact programs, constraint solvers, adversarial critics, or partial formalization. Accepted mathematical knowledge does not become equally cheap: complete proof, understanding, novelty, attribution, significance, and independent expert review remain separate scarce obligations.

The author's own formulation is:

> Mathematical experimentation itself is becoming dramatically cheaper, while proof, understanding, novelty and independent review remain scarce.

The article treats the durable output of research as more than a final theorem: it includes scoped failed routes, eliminated search regions, reductions, counterexamples, formal artifacts, dependencies, provenance, novelty status, known attacks, and explanations.

## Case 1: Hadamard matrix of order 668

The target is a `668 × 668` matrix with entries `+1/-1` whose distinct rows are orthogonal. A candidate is easy to check by exact integer arithmetic: multiply it by its transpose and require diagonal entries of `668` and all off-diagonal entries of `0`.

The author reports:

- `668` is the smallest unresolved order in Epoch AI's current catalogue.
- A naive space contains `2^446224` possible matrices.
- Known construction families reduce the task to smaller structured searches, including sequence constructions derived from `668 = 4 × 167`, Legendre pairs, Williamson-type families, cocyclic constructions, and difference families.
- The main structured route still leaves a search space described as `2^334`, too large for enumeration.
- End-to-end construction and exact verification were calibrated on known smaller orders `28` and `36`.
- Five standard nonexistence routes were audited: Leung–Schmidt field descent, ideal factorization with 2-adic valuation, Hasse–Minkowski, multiplier theorems, and Bruck–Ryser–Chowla. The article reports that none supplied an obstruction at these parameters.
- The project closed `44` precisely scoped regions and left `9` open questions, but found neither a matrix nor a nonexistence proof.

Evidence boundary: these counts and search-coverage claims are author-reported project results. This ingestion did not obtain or rerun the repository, verifier, SAT/constraint jobs, or coverage ledger. They are useful as a workflow case, not independent evidence about the mathematical frontier.

## Case 2: Maxwell equilibrium problem

The article discusses recent work on critical points of potentials generated by positive point charges and an author-generated candidate theorem for three charges.

The source correctly distinguishes three different statuses:

1. **Published/preprint state of the field:** a July 2026 paper refutes Maxwell's proposed general `(k - 1)^2` bound using five positive charges with at least 24 nondegenerate critical points.
2. **Published/preprint upper bound:** another July 2026 paper improves the upper bound for three positive charges from 12 to 6 for every positive Riesz exponent.
3. **Author's candidate:** a proposed bound of at most 4 nondegenerate equilibria for three positive charges, with a conventional proof draft, exact checks on selected inputs, and only the algebraic core partially formalized in Lean.

The candidate rewrites the physical problem using barycentric coordinates and a related surface. It attempts to rule out two local minima using endpoint curvature formulas, a moment inequality, and a planar index count. The author repeatedly states that any bridge in the quantified geometric, analytic, or topological chain may contain an error and that specialist review is required.

### Primary-source checks performed during ingestion

- [The Maxwell Conjecture is False](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27197) states in its abstract that five point charges yield at least 24 nondegenerate critical points, thereby refuting the proposed general bound. Its disclosure says the construction idea was suggested by GPT-5.6 Sol and that the authors verified the mathematical details.
- [From 12 to 6: Sharpening the Three-Charge Bound in Maxwell’s Problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28785) states in its abstract and Theorem 1.1 that three positive point charges have at most 6 nondegenerate equilibria for every positive exponent. Its acknowledgements state that parts were developed with Claude assistance and independently verified by the authors.
- These checks confirm the article's description of the two July papers. They do **not** verify the article author's four-equilibrium candidate.

## Research harness described by the author

The author used GPT-5.6 Sol, parallel agents, exact-arithmetic programs, adversarial critics, literature search, constraint systems, and Lean. The reported loop was:

1. state a precise claim;
2. derive consequences;
3. test small or finite cases exactly;
4. ask a fresh-context critic to attack weak points;
5. formalize the portions where machine checking adds confidence;
6. update a status ledger;
7. keep, revise, or discard the idea.

Fresh-context critics received an artifact and adversarial checklist rather than the full conversation. The author says a fuller release bundle should preserve model configuration, prompts, code versions, and commit hashes.

The article's key validation distinction is:

- exact arithmetic can decisively reject or accept a finite candidate against a complete predicate;
- Lean can certify only the formalized obligations;
- literature search can help establish prior art but not independently prove novelty;
- no single partial gate validates every bridge in a universal theorem or decides significance.

## Related external evidence checked

### AlphaEvolve

Google DeepMind's official [AlphaEvolve announcement](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/) says the system combines model-generated programs with automated evaluators and an evolutionary database. It reports application to more than 50 open mathematical problems, rediscovery of state-of-the-art solutions in roughly 75% of cases, and improvement in roughly 20%.

Boundary: these are DeepMind's own reported results. The durable point is narrower: the approach is suited to domains where candidate programs can be executed and scored automatically. The percentages are source-specific, not a general benchmark for AI mathematical discovery.

### OpenAI unit-distance result

OpenAI's official [announcement](https://openai.com/index/model-disproves-discrete-geometry-conjecture/) reports that an internal model produced a counterexample to a longstanding unit-distance conjecture and that external mathematicians checked the proof and wrote a companion paper.

Boundary: this is a provider announcement plus linked mathematical records, not evidence that autonomous systems generally solve open problems. It supports the article's narrower distinction between model generation and subsequent human digestion, verification, and contextualization.

### “LLMs can't jump”

The article cites Tom Zahavy's ICML 2026 position paper, *LLMs can't jump*, to distinguish induction, deduction, and abduction. The OpenReview PDF endpoint was blocked by a browser verification page during this ingestion, so the paper's full argument was not independently reviewed here. The source itself labels it a position paper and limits the analogy, especially for mathematics.

## Learning and expertise

The author reports that repeated model explanations across algebraic, geometric, numerical, and intuitive representations accelerated entry into unfamiliar mathematics. He calls the outcome “working literacy rather than expertise” and stresses that following an argument is not equivalent to reconstructing or validating it.

The article connects this to two risks:

- fluency can be mistaken for understanding;
- automating literature exercises, modest lemmas, and first proofs may remove work through which students learn research judgment and become future reviewers.

This is a reflective and institutional argument, not a causal result established by the weekend experiment.

## Institutional implications proposed by the source

The article argues that abundant conjectures, proof candidates, counterexamples, and partial formal developments cannot be managed as chat transcripts or undifferentiated PDFs. A useful record should include:

- normalized claim and explicit assumptions;
- status and novelty labels;
- exact scope of computation;
- formal artifacts and dependencies;
- human/machine provenance;
- known attacks, failures, and open obligations;
- plain-language explanation;
- consent and attribution boundaries for unpublished ideas.

Search should operate over claims and dependencies, not only keywords. Well-scoped failures should be searchable so eliminated branches and failed constructions narrow future work rather than being repeated.

The article also argues that credit should follow the dependency and interpretation chain. Digesting, checking, correcting, and explaining generated mathematics may be more valuable than producing the first candidate proof.

These are design proposals and normative judgments. The article does not demonstrate a deployed claim registry, governance model, or incentive system.

## What the source supports

- A first-person case showing how parallel generation plus exact and partial formal checks can increase experimental throughput.
- A clear contrast between a finite candidate with a complete deterministic verifier and a universal theorem with only partial formal coverage.
- A reusable warning that verifier authority is limited to its encoded contract.
- A case for preserving scoped negative results, provenance, dependencies, status, and review obligations.
- A plausible institutional forecast that review and curation can become the bottleneck when generation becomes cheap.

## What it does not establish

- Existence or nonexistence of a Hadamard matrix of order 668.
- Correctness or novelty of the proposed four-equilibrium Maxwell theorem.
- That current LLMs cannot perform abduction in any formal or universal sense.
- General rates of AI success on open mathematics.
- Causal evidence that AI use weakens researcher training or future peer review.
- That a particular claim graph, formalization stack, model, or multi-agent architecture should become a Hermes default.
