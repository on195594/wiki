## Laws

### Architecture

- [[hyrums-law|Hyrum's Law]] — With a sufficient number of API users, all observable behaviors of your system will be depended on by somebody. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/hyrums-law/))
- [[galls-law|Gall's Law]] — A complex system that works is invariably found to have evolved from a simple system that worked. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/galls-law/))
- [[law-of-leaky-abstractions|The Law of Leaky Abstractions]] — All non-trivial abstractions, to some degree, are leaky. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/law-of-leaky-abstractions/))
- [[teslers-law|Tesler's Law (Conservation of Complexity)]] — Every application has an inherent amount of irreducible complexity that can only be shifted, not eliminated. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/teslers-law/))
- [[cap-theorem|CAP Theorem]] — A distributed system can guarantee only two of: consistency, availability, and partition tolerance. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/cap-theorem/))
- [[second-system-effect|Second-System Effect]] — Small, successful systems tend to be followed by overengineered, bloated replacements. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/second-system-effect/))
- [[fallacies-of-distributed-computing|Fallacies of Distributed Computing]] — A set of eight false assumptions that new distributed system designers often make. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/fallacies-of-distributed-computing/))
- [[law-of-unintended-consequences|Law of Unintended Consequences]] — Whenever you change a complex system, expect surprise. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/law-of-unintended-consequences/))
- [[zawinskis-law|Zawinski's Law]] — Every program attempts to expand until it can read mail. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/zawinskis-law/))

### Teams

- [[conways-law|Conway's Law]] — Organizations design systems that mirror their own communication structure. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/conways-law/))
- [[brooks-law|Brooks's Law]] — Adding manpower to a late software project makes it later. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/brooks-law/))
- [[dunbars-number|Dunbar's Number]] — There is a cognitive limit of about 150 stable relationships one person can maintain. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/dunbars-number/))
- [[ringelmann-effect|The Ringelmann Effect]] — Individual productivity decreases as group size increases. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/ringelmann-effect/))
- [[prices-law|Price's Law]] — The square root of the total number of participants does 50% of the work. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/prices-law/))
- [[putts-law|Putt's Law]] — Those who understand technology don't manage it, and those who manage it don't understand it. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/putts-law/))
- [[peter-principle|Peter Principle]] — In a hierarchy, every employee tends to rise to their level of incompetence. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/peter-principle/))
- [[bus-factor|Bus Factor]] — The minimum number of team members whose loss would put the project in serious trouble. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/bus-factor/))
- [[dilbert-principle|Dilbert Principle]] — Companies tend to promote incompetent employees to management to limit the damage they can do. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/dilbert-principle/))

### Planning

- [[premature-optimization|Premature Optimization (Knuth's Optimization Principle)]] — Premature optimization is the root of all evil. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/premature-optimization/))
- [[parkinsons-law|Parkinson's Law]] — Work expands to fill the time available for its completion. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/parkinsons-law/))
- [[ninety-ninety-rule|The Ninety-Ninety Rule]] — The first 90% of the code accounts for the first 90% of development time; the remaining 10% accounts for the other 90%. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/ninety-ninety-rule/))
- [[hofstadters-law|Hofstadter's Law]] — It always takes longer than you expect, even when you take into account Hofstadter's Law. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/hofstadters-law/))
- [[goodharts-law|Goodhart's Law]] — When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/goodharts-law/))
- [[gilbs-law|Gilb's Law]] — Anything you need to quantify can be measured in some way better than not measuring it. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/gilbs-law/))

### Quality

- [[boy-scout-rule|The Boy Scout Rule]] — Leave the code better than you found it. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/boy-scout-rule/))
- [[murphys-law|Murphy's Law / Sod's Law]] — Anything that can go wrong will go wrong. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/murphys-law/))
- [[postels-law|Postel's Law]] — Be conservative in what you do, be liberal in what you accept from others. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/postels-law/))
- [[broken-windows-theory|Broken Windows Theory]] — Don't leave broken windows (bad designs, wrong decisions, or poor code) unrepaired. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/broken-windows-theory/))
- [[technical-debt|Technical Debt]] — Technical Debt is everything that slows us down when developing software. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/technical-debt/))
- [[linuss-law|Linus's Law]] — Given enough eyeballs, all bugs are shallow. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/linuss-law/))
- [[kernighans-law|Kernighan's Law]] — Debugging is twice as hard as writing the code in the first place. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/kernighans-law/))
- [[testing-pyramid|Testing Pyramid]] — A project should have many fast unit tests, fewer integration tests, and only a small number of UI tests. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/testing-pyramid/))
- [[pesticide-paradox|Pesticide Paradox]] — Repeatedly running the same tests becomes less effective over time. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/pesticide-paradox/))
- [[lehmans-laws|Lehman's Laws of Software Evolution]] — Software that reflects the real world must evolve, and that evolution has predictable limits. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/lehmans-laws/))
- [[sturgeons-law|Sturgeon's Law]] — 90% of everything is crap. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/sturgeons-law/))

### Scale

- [[amdahls-law|Amdahl's Law]] — The speedup from parallelization is limited by the fraction of work that cannot be parallelized. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/amdahls-law/))
- [[gustafsons-law|Gustafson's Law]] — It is possible to achieve significant speedup in parallel processing by increasing the problem size. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/gustafsons-law/))
- [[metcalfes-law|Metcalfe's Law]] — The value of a network is proportional to the square of the number of users. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/metcalfes-law/))

### Design

- [[yagni|YAGNI (You Aren't Gonna Need It)]] — Don't add functionality until it is necessary. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/yagni/))
- [[dry-principle|DRY (Don't Repeat Yourself)]] — Every piece of knowledge must have a single, unambiguous, authoritative representation. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/dry-principle/))
- [[kiss-principle|KISS (Keep It Simple, Stupid)]] — Designs and systems should be as simple as possible. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/kiss-principle/))
- [[solid-principles|SOLID Principles]] — Five main guidelines that enhance software design, making code more maintainable and scalable. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/solid-principles/))
- [[law-of-demeter|Law of Demeter]] — An object should only interact with its immediate friends, not strangers. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/law-of-demeter/))
- [[principle-of-least-astonishment|Principle of Least Astonishment]] — Software and interfaces should behave in a way that least surprises users and other developers. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/principle-of-least-astonishment/))

### Decisions

- [[dunning-kruger-effect|Dunning-Kruger Effect]] — The less you know about something, the more confident you tend to be. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/dunning-kruger-effect/))
- [[hanlons-razor|Hanlon's Razor]] — Never attribute to malice that which is adequately explained by stupidity or carelessness. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/hanlons-razor/))
- [[occams-razor|Occam's Razor]] — The simplest explanation is often the most accurate one. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/occams-razor/))
- [[sunk-cost-fallacy|Sunk Cost Fallacy]] — Sticking with a choice because you've invested time or energy in it, even when walking away helps you. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/sunk-cost-fallacy/))
- [[map-is-not-the-territory|The Map Is Not the Territory]] — Our representations of reality are not the same as reality itself. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/map-is-not-the-territory/))
- [[confirmation-bias|Confirmation Bias]] — A tendency to favor information that supports our existing beliefs or ideas. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/confirmation-bias/))
- [[hype-cycle-amaras-law|The Hype Cycle & Amara's Law]] — We tend to overestimate the effect of a technology in the short run and underestimate the impact in the long run. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/hype-cycle-amaras-law/))
- [[lindy-effect|The Lindy Effect]] — The longer something has been in use, the more likely it is to continue being used. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/lindy-effect/))
- [[first-principles-thinking|First Principles Thinking]] — Breaking a complex problem into its most basic blocks and then building up from there. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/first-principles-thinking/))
- [[inversion|Inversion]] — Solving a problem by considering the opposite outcome and working backward from it. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/inversion/))
- [[pareto-principle|Pareto Principle (80/20 Rule)]] — 80% of the problems result from 20% of the causes. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/pareto-principle/))
- [[cunninghams-law|Cunningham's Law]] — The best way to get the correct answer on the Internet is not to ask a question, it's to post the wrong answer. ([source](https://lawsofsoftwareengineering.com/laws/cunninghams-law/))
